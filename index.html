Module 1

Active vs. Passive Voice in Medical Narratives

Voice Breakdown

  • Active Voice: Focuses on the doer (agent) → Subject + Verb + Object.
    Example: "Andrew injected the medicine."
  • Passive Voice: Focuses on the action or receiver → Object + Form of 'to be' + Past Participle (V3) + by Agent.
    Example: "The medicine was injected by Andrew."
Why Doctors Use Passive Voice: In clinical and scientific writing, passive voice emphasizes the medical procedure or patient condition rather than who performed it, maintaining objective professionalism.

Golden Rules & The Intransitive Trap

  1. The direct object of the active sentence becomes the subject of the passive sentence.
  2. Use the correct tense form of 'to be' + V3 (past participle).
  3. The original subject becomes the agent preceded by 'by' (or is omitted when irrelevant).
Watch Out for Intransitive Verbs: Verbs with no direct object (like wait, pace, arrive, walk) cannot be converted into passive voice.

Practice Challenge: 10 Voice Questions (Based on Birth)

Instructions: Convert active sentences to passive, passive to active, or spot the Intransitive Trap (sentences with no direct object that cannot be converted).
Q1 • Active → Passive

Andrew injected the life-saving medicine into Susan's arm.

Target Passive: The life-saving medicine was injected into Susan's arm by Andrew.
Q2 • Active → Passive

The frightened midwife placed the lifeless infant beneath the bed among sodden newspapers.

Target Passive: The lifeless infant was placed beneath the bed among sodden newspapers by the frightened midwife.
Q3 • Active → Passive?

Joe Morgan paced anxiously up and down outside the house.

Intransitive Trap! Cannot be converted because 'paced' is an intransitive verb with no direct object.
Q4 • Active → Passive

The elderly mother handed a warm cup of tea to Andrew.

Target Passive: A warm cup of tea was handed to Andrew by the elderly mother.
Q5 • Active → Passive

Andrew rubbed the limp infant's body with a rough towel.

Target Passive: The limp infant's body was rubbed with a rough towel by Andrew.
Q6 • Passive → Active

The child was plunged alternately into the icy and steaming baths by the desperate doctor.

Target Active: The desperate doctor plunged the child alternately into the icy and steaming baths.
Q7 • Active → Passive

The midwife whimpered, "It's stillborn."

Target Passive: A frightened whimper of "It's stillborn" was uttered by the midwife.
Q8 • Active → Passive

Andrew smashed the glass ampule with frantic speed.

Target Passive: The glass ampule was smashed with frantic speed by Andrew.
Q9 • Passive → Active

A joyful iridescent bubble of mucus was pushed from the tiny nostril.

Target Active: The tiny nostril pushed out a joyful iridescent bubble of mucus (or: Mucus formed a joyful iridescent bubble at the tiny nostril).
Q10 • Active → Passive?

First-shift miners walked out into the streets at five o'clock.

Intransitive Trap! Cannot be converted because 'walked' is an intransitive verb with no direct object.

Module 2

Degrees of Comparison & Character Evolution

1. Positive Degree

States a base quality without comparison.

Examples: weary, tense, calm, real, desperate

2. Comparative Degree

Compares two states, people, or moments in time (-er or more).

Examples: wearier, tenser, more exhausted, worse

3. Superlative Degree

Marks the extreme highest or lowest degree in a group (-est or most).

Examples: weariest, tensest, most significant, worst

Charting Andrew Manson's Journey on an Adjectival Scale

Tired / Weary → Wearier / More exhausted → Weariest / Most exhausted
Desperate → More desperate → Most desperate
Bad (Irregular) → Worse → Worst
Real → More real / Realer → Most real / Realest

Practice Challenge: 10 Comparison Questions (Based on Birth)

Instructions: Fill in the correct degree of the adjective provided in brackets, complete the scale, or transform the sentence as directed.
Q1 • Fill in the Blank

At midnight, Andrew felt [weary] than he usually did after a long day.

Answer: wearier (or: more weary)
Q2 • Fill in the Blank

Saving the child was the [significant] achievement of Andrew's early medical career in Blaenelly.

Answer: most significant
Q3 • Irregular Form

As the minutes ticked away by the kitchen fire, Andrew's anxiety grew [bad] than before.

Answer: worse
Q4 • Fill in the Blank

The atmosphere in the upstairs bedroom was [tense] than the quiet kitchen downstairs.

Answer: tenser (or: more tense)
Q5 • Superlative

Cronin’s description of the resuscitation scene is one of the [vivid] passages in English literature.

Answer: most vivid
Q6 • Dual Blank

Andrew experienced a [deep] sense of defeat when the baby first appeared stillborn, but his final realization was even [profound].

Answer: deep (or deeper), more profound
Q7 • Transformation

Transform using the superlative degree: "No other moment in Andrew's life was as real as this." → "This was the [real] moment of his life."

Answer: most real / realest
Q8 • Scaling Chain

Complete the adjective scaling chain: Positive: desperate → Comparative: [ ? ] → Superlative: [ ? ].

Answer: Comparative: more desperate • Superlative: most desperate
Q9 • Rewrite

Rewrite using the comparative degree: "The midwife was hopeless, but Andrew was determined." → "Andrew was [determined] than the midwife."

Answer: more determined
Q10 • Identify Root

Identify the degree of comparison in the final sentence: "I've done something real at last." What is the positive root adjective?

Answer: Positive degree • Root adjective: "real"
